EPS Scenario Lab

Build a small materials evidence graph, sparsify it with Evidence-Preserving Sparsification (EPS) or a generic method under the same node budget, and see whether the answer is still correct, and whether anyone could still check it. The graphs and values here are illustrative and computed live in your browser; they are not experimental output.

2What happens

  1. The graph is built. Each MOF gets claims (C) with evidence (E), a level of theory (K) and a DFT/ML qualification (Q), linked to shared provenance (P) and derivation (D) nodes. Missing values become exclusion records (X). ML predictions that disagree by more than 1 eV become contradicting claims. Grey nodes are context.
  2. Audit units are formed: one support unit per comparable claim, one contradiction unit per competing prediction, and one exclusion record per MOF without a value.
  3. A budget k is set: k = max(|C|, ⌊β·|N(q)|⌋). Every method may keep at most k nodes.
  4. Two readers answer from what is left: the conservative reader abstains if any kept claim lacks its condition or qualification; the permissive reader assumes missing information is fine.
  5. The audit check asks whether every unit survived complete, so the answer could be replayed from the kept graph alone.
